## DJ Moore — WR, BUF, bye 7  (player_id: 4983)

- **Role:** starter (Buffalo's WR1)
- **Situation:** Traded in March from Chicago to Buffalo along with a 2026 fifth-rounder for a 2026 second-rounder, after the Bears consolidated their offense around Luther Burden III, Rome Odunze and Colston Loveland. Moore is reunited with Joe Brady, now Buffalo's head coach, who coached him in Carolina. He'll lead the Bills in targets — the competition is Khalil Shakir (about 6.3 targets/game over two seasons), a returning Dalton Kincaid, and James Cook out of the backfield. Behind him the WR room has been banged up (Shakir undisclosed, Keon Coleman foot, Skyler Bell lower body). 2025 was the worst season of his career: WR41 in PPG, roughly 9.7-10.0 PPR points per game, a 14-16% target share (career low outside his rookie year), 1.26-1.42 yards per route run (career low), and 80 targets snapping a six-year streak of 100+.
- **Health:** Available. He rolled his right ankle late in the preseason opener vs. Carolina on Aug. 15; Joe Brady said he was already scheduled to come out and Moore reported he was fine. He was limited the following Tuesday, back to full team drills in pads by Thursday, and rested along with other starters in the preseason finale. Expected to start Week 1 vs. Houston.
- **Risk:** The Allen Robinson comparison is the specific fear — a receiver whose target-earning metrics collapsed at 29, who then moved to a better offense and simply didn't recover. Buffalo also isn't a high-volume passing team: Josh Allen averaged 28.75 attempts per game last season and has been under 29.0 in both years under Brady. Even a 25% target share caps him near 120 targets, which in this offense projects to roughly 75/1,000/6 — good, not a difference-maker.
- **Draft note:** In 0.5 PPR the reception-light profile hurts less than in full PPR, and Allen's TD equity helps. He's a WR2 bet on rebound, not a WR1. Worth taking if he falls to the WR20s; avoid paying for the upside case.
- **Confidence:** medium
