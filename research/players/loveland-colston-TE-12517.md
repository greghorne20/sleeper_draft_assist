## Colston Loveland — TE, CHI, bye 10  (player_id: 12517)

- **Role:** starter
- **Situation:** Chicago's clear No. 1 tight end and, by several projections, the team's leading target earner. He's listed as a starter alongside Cole Kmet on the unofficial depth chart. His rookie year was a slow burn — 11 catches through Week 8 — that turned into 58-713-6 on 82 targets, and from Week 9 through the divisional round he caught 59 of 89 for 790 and six scores in 12 games. Over that stretch he was the half-PPR TE7 in points per game, with the second-most yards per route run among TEs (1.84, behind only George Kittle) and the seventh-most targets per route run. The offseason change that matters: D.J. Moore is off the roster (traded to Buffalo), and Ben Johnson has a track record of featuring tight ends, as he did with Sam LaPorta in Detroit. ESPN projects 118 targets. Luther Burden III (groin) and Rome Odunze are the competing pass-catchers.
- **Health:** No current injury. He sat out one August practice along with several other skill starters, and had a one-game DNP as a rookie with a hip injury. Burden's groin issue kept him out for part of camp.
- **Risk:** Price, and Caleb Williams. He's going in the fourth round as roughly the TE2–3, so he needs a genuine 22–25% target share to return the pick — and that requires Williams to consistently work the middle of the field, which was not a strength early last year. Burden getting healthy and Odunze taking a step both eat into the ceiling.
- **Draft note:** In a single-TE, half-PPR league with no TE premium, paying a fourth-round pick for a tight end only works if he actually hits the TE1–2 outcome; the profile supports it, but the price removes the margin.
- **Confidence:** high
