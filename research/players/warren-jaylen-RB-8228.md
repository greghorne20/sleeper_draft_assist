## Jaylen Warren — RB, PIT, bye 9  (player_id: 8228)

- **Role:** starter, but in a genuine committee — listed RB1 on Pittsburgh's initial and first preseason depth charts ahead of Rico Dowdle
- **Situation:** Coming off his best fantasy season (RB18 in PPG) with career highs of 211 carries, 958 rushing yards, six rushing TDs and eight total TDs, plus 40 catches for 333 yards. He was extremely efficient — sixth in missed tackle rate, ninth in yards after contact per attempt, third in yards per route run. Kenneth Gainwell, who took most of the passing-down work last year, left for Tampa Bay, which should return receiving volume to Warren. But new head coach Mike McCarthy brought in Rico Dowdle, his former Cowboys back, and the two alternated first-team reps through camp with McCarthy declining to name a starter. Kaleb Johnson, Lew Nichols, Travis Homer and Eli Heidenreich round out the room. The receiving corps is DK Metcalf, Michael Pittman Jr. and Roman Wilson. Warren is in year one of a two-year, ~$12M extension.
- **Health:** No current injury reported. He played 16 regular-season games in 2025 (missed one earlier in that year with a Week 4 injury). Nothing lingering surfaced in searches.
- **Risk:** Dowdle. The two are similar players with similar résumés and neither has proven to be a true lead back — the most likely outcome is an annoying, unpredictable split rather than either man breaking out. Add that Pittsburgh is expected to throw fewer passes to running backs this year and that Warren's average target depth was minus-2.5 yards, and you get a back whose fantasy value rests on touch volume that isn't guaranteed week to week.
- **Draft note:** RB27-ish ADP is roughly right. In 0.5 PPR his 40-catch receiving role is worth about 20 points over a season, so he's a flex-plus rather than a set-and-forget RB2. Fine as your RB3 in the middle rounds; the Dowdle handcuff scenario cuts both ways.
- **Confidence:** medium
