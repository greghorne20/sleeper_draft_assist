## Luther Burden III — WR, CHI, bye 10  (player_id: 12519)
- **Role:** starter — expected full-time role in 2026, but he was never full-time in 2025
- **Situation:** Burden's rookie usage and his rookie efficiency point in opposite directions. Efficiency: 2.79 yards per route run, which is elite — fourth in the NFL in YPRR by one accounting, behind only Puka Nacua, Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Zay Flowers. Usage: 234 routes, a 12.8% target share, 47 catches on 60 targets and two TDs; under 30% of snaps in every game for the first two months, and he never exceeded 65% of snaps in any of his final three games despite an 8-138-1 explosion vs. San Francisco in Week 17. From Week 10 on he averaged nearly 64 yards per game, a 1,000-yard pace. What changed: D.J. Moore was traded to Buffalo (85 targets, 14.8% share, and — more importantly — the team's WR lead in snaps at 84.8% and routes at 81.2%), and Olamide Zaccheaus (11.3% share) left in free agency. Chicago added no meaningful receiver help, drafting only WR Zavion Thomas and TE Sam Roush in round 3. Ben Johnson has been loud in his praise, publicly saying "I'm buying Luther Burden stock." Target competition is Rome Odunze and Colston Loveland, who broke out as a top-five fantasy TE in the second half of 2025.
- **Health:** Groin injury suffered Aug. 8, which cost him the entire preseason — the timeline stretched from "a little bit of time" to about a month. He returned to individual drills Aug. 26, then to a short, light practice, and Ben Johnson said Sunday (~Aug. 30) that he "looks 100%." He's on track to play Week 1.
- **Risk:** He remains the third option. Even with Moore gone, Odunze and Loveland are ahead of him in the pecking order, and the Bears showed last year that even after a monster game they'd put him back in a part-time role. The specific failure is a 15-16% target share and a frustrating WR3 finish with two or three spike weeks. His rookie week-to-week floor was ugly — negative receiving yards in three separate games.
- **Draft note:** ADP around WR25 with analysts ranking him WR17-22, so opinion is split hard. He's a boom pick, not a floor pick, and half-PPR reduces the cushion a low-catch-volume player would get in full PPR. Worth taking around pick 50-55 as a WR3/flex; I wouldn't pay a round 3 price on offseason coach quotes.
- **Confidence:** medium — the situation and health are well documented, but his projected snap share and target share for 2026 are genuinely unknown, and that's the entire question with him.
