## Rome Odunze — WR, CHI, bye 10  (player_id: 11620)

- **Role:** starter (locked into every two-receiver set alongside Luther Burden III)
- **Situation:** Odunze led the Bears with 90 targets in 2025 and turned them into 661 yards, and through Weeks 1–12 he was the WR21 in points per game with a 22.4–23.7% target share — while dealing with a 68.3% catchable target rate, 96th of 127 qualifying receivers. Then a foot stress fracture ended the run. This offseason Chicago traded DJ Moore to Buffalo (85 targets, 14.8% share) and let Olamide Zaccheaus walk (11.3% share), adding no meaningful pass-catchers beyond third-rounders Zavion Thomas and Sam Roush. That leaves Odunze competing with Burden and Colston Loveland for a concentrated target pie in year three of Caleb Williams and year two of Ben Johnson.
- **Health:** The foot is not currently a concern. Odunze played through a stress fracture in 2025 and the injury changed the bone structure in his foot; his June "new normal" comments were widely read as an admission he isn't right, which he has since walked back. He needed no offseason surgery, and The Athletic's Dan Wiederer reported no lingering effects across 15 camp practices. He caught an 18-yard pass on the Bears' second snap of the preseason.
- **Risk:** He finishes third in this target hierarchy. Multiple analysts rank Burden and Loveland ahead of him, and the efficiency case against him is real — 1.72 yards per route run (38th), targeted on 23.4% of routes (28th), sub-54% catch rate in both NFL seasons, 14.3 career aDOT with 41.4% of targets 15+ yards downfield. That deep-heavy profile plus a low catch rate is the worst combination for a half-PPR floor. The foot is a background risk rather than a live one.
- **Draft note:** Priced as a WR3 with WR2 upside, going around the early sixth round. That's about right in 0.5 PPR — the boom weeks are real but the catch volume won't carry you on the quiet ones. Fine at cost; don't overexpose across multiple rosters.
- **Confidence:** high
