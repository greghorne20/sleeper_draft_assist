## Jaylen Waddle — WR, DEN, bye 10  (player_id: 7526)
- **Role:** starter — acquired to be the focal point of Denver's passing game
- **Situation:** Waddle was traded from Miami to Denver in March 2026 (Broncos sent picks 30, 94 and 130; got Waddle plus pick 111). He's signed through 2028 on a three-year, $84.75M deal. The situational change is the entire case for him: Miami was 24th in points per drive and 26th in offensive EPA over the past two seasons and 29th in neutral pass rate; Denver led the NFL in pass attempts last year and was fourth in neutral pass rate. He finished 2025 with 64 catches for 910 yards and six TDs (WR25, 12.1 PPG) on a 7-10 Miami team that benched Tua and lost Tyreek Hill in Week 4 — his catches per game fell from 5.3 across his first three years to 3.9 across the last two, which reads as a usage collapse rather than a decline. His underlying metrics stayed strong: 12th in targets per route run, 12th in YPRR, 7th in first downs per route run, 12th in separation score, and 12th-best PFF receiving grade among WRs. He beat Courtland Sutton in nearly all of those. Denver's room: Sutton (31, 74-1,017-7 last year, efficiency slipping), Troy Franklin, Marvin Mims, Pat Bryant and TE Evan Engram. Sean Payton handed playcalling to new OC Davis Webb.
- **Health:** No current injury designation found. He played in Denver's Aug. 21 preseason game vs. Green Bay. FantasyPros carried an undated camp note that Waddle (muscle strain) was expected to miss 4-5 days — I could not date or resolve that item, and it does not appear to have affected his availability.
- **Risk:** Target density in a room where Payton has historically spread it around, plus the quarterback. Bo Nix isn't a great deep thrower, so Waddle's usage may skew short/intermediate — fine for volume, weaker for the explosive plays that are his edge. And Denver's opening schedule is brutal: Chiefs, Jaguars, Rams, 49ers, Chargers, Seahawks in the first six weeks. The pick fails if Sutton keeps the target and red-zone lead by default and Waddle settles in at 6 targets a game.
- **Draft note:** ADP around 49.6, roughly WR22-30 depending on source. That's a discount for a player with a 100-catch season on his résumé walking into the league's highest-volume passing offense. Half-PPR is mildly less kind to him than full PPR given the catch-heavy profile, but at that price he's a strong WR2/flex target in round 4-5 — probably the best value-to-price ratio in this batch.
- **Confidence:** high on role and situation; medium on health, given the unresolved camp muscle-strain note.
