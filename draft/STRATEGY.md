# Strategy reference — 12-team half-PPR, Season 21 (2026)

The reasoning, numbers and citations behind `draft/PLAYBOOK.md`. The playbook is what you run
during the draft; this is what you consult when a decision is close, when a rule needs
justifying, or when the board breaks in a way the playbook's defaults do not cover.

Compiled September 5, 2026. Primary ADP source: FantasyFootballCalculator 12-team half-PPR
(2,879 mock drafts, Aug 31–Sep 5, 2026). Cross-referenced with FantasyPros consensus, RotoWire,
CBS, ESPN, Sharp Football, PFF, NBC/Rotoworld, Draft Sharks, STACKED Fantasy.

**All ADP snapshots are pre-Week-1 2026 and drift. Treat any ADP here as stale after ~Sept 10.**

---

## The one-paragraph version

This is a skill-position-only, 7-starter, 6-bench, half-PPR, 1-QB league with third-round
reversal. The correct default is **Hero/Anchor RB or balanced BPA**: secure one elite RB (or an
early tier-1 WR), attack the deep WR pool, take an elite TE only at value, and **wait on QB until
~R8–11** because the QB1-to-QB12 gap is only ~3.9 PPG under this QB-suppressing scoring. Draft by
tiers, not ranks. The biggest positional value edges by VORP are, in order:
**elite RB > elite WR > elite TE (~5.5 PPG over TE12) > QB (smallest edge).**

---

## 1. VORP and replacement level for this exact config

Value Over Replacement = player projected points − replacement points at that position.
Replacement = the last startable player given league-wide starting demand.

**Replacement baselines** (12 teams; 1QB / 2RB / 2WR / 1TE / 1FLEX):

| Pos | Starters leaguewide | Replacement rank | Half-PPR proj (STACKED, Sep 4–5 '26) |
|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 12 | QB12 (stream ≈ QB15–18) | ~296 (QB18 ≈ 279) |
| RB | 24 + ~half of 12 FLEX | ≈ RB30 | ~142 |
| WR | 24 + ~half of 12 FLEX | ≈ WR30 | ~155–160 |
| TE | 12 | TE12 (points-based ≈ TE12) | ~120–127 |

**VORP of the elite anchors:**

| Player | Pos | Proj | Replacement | VORP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jahmyr Gibbs | RB1 | 300 | 142 | **+158** |
| Ja'Marr Chase | WR1 | 257 | 157 | **+100** |
| Brock Bowers | TE1 | 203 | 120 | **+83** |
| Josh Allen | QB1 | 362 | 296 | **+66** (vs QB18 baseline: +83) |

**The four takeaways to encode:**
- Elite RB carries the single largest VORP → an elite RB is the highest-value R1 pick.
- Elite WR is a close second and there are more of them → **WR is where you win the middle rounds.**
- Elite TE (Bowers/McBride) has a **larger edge over its replacement (~5.5 PPG / +83–93 season
  points) than QB1 does** → an elite TE is worth an early pick; a mid TE is not.
- **QB has the smallest edge** (QB1−QB12 ≈ 3.9 PPG; QB2−QB18 spans only ~2.8 PPG per
  RotoWire/STACKED) → wait on QB.

> **Caveat.** These full-season projections come primarily from one source (STACKED Fantasy,
> Sep 4–5 2026). FantasyPros consensus runs ~10–12% higher on the absolute RB scale (Gibbs 334
> vs 300). Use one source's full curve for internal consistency and **trust the VORP ordering,
> not the absolute point values.** Re-sort each position strictly by projected points before
> computing VORP — some published "ranks" are ADP-ordered and slightly non-monotonic.

## 2. Structural approaches — when each fits

| Approach | Definition | Fit for THIS league | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hero/Anchor RB** | 1 stud RB in R1, then WRs/TE, RBs later | **DEFAULT best fit.** Hedges RB fragility while exploiting WR depth. "Extreme Hero RB is the new Zero RB" (Theo Gremminger, Jul '26). | Strong |
| **Robust RB** | 2–3 RBs in the first 3–4 rounds | Viable from early slots (1–4) if RB tiers 1–2 fall; half-PPR keeps RB usable | Situational |
| **Zero RB** | No RB until ~R5–7, load WR/TE | Harder in 2026. FantasySixPack: "For the 2026 season, this strategy is not as appealing as it has been. There looks to be a dozen or so legitimate workhorse running backs that you really want to have at least one on your roster." Only if WR/TE value forces it, or from mid slots. | Weaker in '26 |
| **Modified Zero RB** | 1 mid-round RB anchor, rest WR | Reasonable compromise from mid slots (5–8) | Moderate |
| **Balanced / BPA** | Take best tiered value each pick | Excellent with a tier-based board; 3RR + deep bench rewards flexibility | Strong |
| **Elite QB** | Pay up for Allen ~R3 | **Not recommended** — QB edge too small under this scoring | Weak here |
| **Late-Round QB** | Wait to R8–11 for QB | **Recommended default** | Strong |

**Half-PPR note:** versus full PPR, RB retains more relative value (fewer PPR points inflating
WRs), so lean slightly more RB than a full-PPR Zero-RB build would. The countervailing 2026
signal (FantasyPros, Aug 15 2026): "15 of the top 25 non-QBs in full-PPR scoring were running
backs" in 2025 — "That's 60% of the group" — which is why more workhorse RBs are going early and
why the pure Zero-RB case "has gotten a little murkier."

## 3. Roster construction across 13 picks (no K/DST)

Pick **one** end-of-draft shape based on how the board breaks:

- **Balanced (default):** 1 QB, 6 RB, 5 WR, 1 TE — or 2 QB, 5 RB, 5 WR, 1 TE
- **Elite-TE build:** 1 QB, 5 RB, 5 WR, 2 TE (only if you land Bowers/McBride early)
- **WR-heavy (Zero/Modified):** 1 QB, 4 RB, 7 WR, 1 TE

Rules of thumb:
- **Minimum 5 RB** — no K/DST means you carry RB insurance you'd otherwise stream, and RB
  injuries are the number-one source of league-winners.
- **1 TE if elite; 2 TE if you punted** (stream/committee the position — carry a second dart).
- **1 QB if you grab a top-8; 2 QB if you truly punt** to R11–13 (draft two you can platoon by
  matchup).
- **Never fewer than ~10 combined RB+WR.**

## 4. The QB question

- **Gap data:** QB1 (Allen ~362, ~21.3 PPG) − QB12 (~296, ~17.4) ≈ **3.9 PPG**;
  QB1 − QB18 ≈ **4.9 PPG**. RotoWire: the QB6–QB12 gap was only ~2.2 PPG in 2025.
  FantasyPros: 2025 QB1 22.0 vs QB12 18.0 PPG.
- **Verdict: late-round QB.** Wait until ~R8–11; take two shots at upside if punting. Target
  rushing profiles. FantasyPros and FanSided both call QB "an extremely low-value slot" in 2026.
- **The only exception:** if Allen falls to ~R4–5 (well past his ~pick 31 ADP) the value can
  justify it — or if you reach the QB2 tier and every alternative skill player is a clear tier down.
- **Sweet-spot targets (ADP):** Bo Nix (~QB15), Caleb Williams, **Brock Purdy** (major value per
  CBS/RotoWire), Trevor Lawrence, Jaxson Dart, Kyler Murray, Jared Goff, Baker Mayfield.
  Late darts: Malik Willis (100+ rush attempts projected), Sam Darnold.

**Why rushing QBs specifically:** this league scores rush yards at 0.1/yd against pass yards at
0.04/yd (10 vs 25 yards per point) and rush TDs at 6 against pass TDs at 4. Designed runs are
worth **2.5x** their passing equivalent in yardage and 1.5x in TDs. Prioritise legs — Allen,
Jackson, Daniels, Hurts, Nix, Murray, Dart, Lawrence, Willis — over pure pocket arms.

## 5. The TE question

- **Two-tier reality.** Bowers (proj ~203) and McBride (~187) sit far above the field. McBride
  scored 252.9 half-PPR points in 2025 — "almost 90 more than the next closest tight end" — on an
  NFL-record 126 receptions (1,239 yds, 11 TDs) per FantasyData; Draft Sharks notes he "averaged
  3.9 more points per game than any other TE." **TE1−TE12 ≈ 5.5 PPG — the steepest
  elite-vs-replacement drop of any position.**
- **Decision rule.** Draft Bowers or McBride if they fall to your R3–4 pick (ADP ~40–42, i.e.
  late 3rd / early 4th). Otherwise **wait and target the value tier**: Loveland, Warren (young
  studs, R5–7), then Kittle (Achilles recovery) / LaPorta / Kraft / Fannin / Pitts.
- **Do NOT pay a mid-round pick for a middling TE.** The edge evaporates below the top 2–4. If
  you miss the top 4, punt to a late committee TE (Kelce, Andrews, Likely, Goedert, Kincaid) and
  carry two.

## 6. The RB "dead zone" — 2026 status

- **Definition:** the middle rounds (~R3–7) where RB cost historically outran production and WRs
  outscored them.
- **2026 status: it still exists but has shifted later (now ~R7–10) and is more a tier break than
  a fixed set of rounds.** Yahoo/FantasyPros PAR analysis (RB30 baseline) shows the minimum has
  "dithered around R7–10." The market got smarter and committees are priced in; the 2025 data
  (60% of top-25 non-QBs were RBs) shows the top of the position produced. **The trap is now
  specifically volume-only backs in poor offenses, not all mid-round RBs.**
- **Rule:** in R4–7, only take a RB whose **role + price + path to points all beat the WR on the
  board.** Otherwise take the WR. Target volume-secure or ambiguous-upside backs — David
  Montgomery (~12.3 half-PPR PPG projected), Quinshon Judkins, Bhayshul Tuten, TreVeyon Henderson.

Players sitting in the dead zone are flagged `dead_zone` in `draft/board.md`.

## 7. Handcuffing (the 6-bench rule)

FTN: "I consider five or fewer bench spots a short bench… I'm generally not interested in
handcuffing." **With 6 benches and no K/DST you have slightly more room — but still handcuff
selectively.**

- **Prefer handcuffs with standalone value** (they play even if the starter is healthy):
  Blake Corum (LAR, behind Kyren), Rico Dowdle (PIT, "1-2 punch"), Tyler Allgeier (ARI),
  MarShawn Lloyd (GB), Mike Washington Jr. (LV, behind a fragile Jeanty), Isiah Pacheco (DET,
  behind Gibbs), Zach Charbonnet (SEA, elite pure handcuff — 21.9 half-PPR PPG in games Walker
  missed).
- **Handcuff YOUR OWN fragile stud first** — e.g. if you draft McCaffrey, grab Jordan James.
  Speculative handcuffs of other people's backs are worth it only late, and only behind true
  bell-cows.

The board carries a `handcuff_for` link on the pairings the research names explicitly, so you can
look up the backup for a starter you own.

## 8. Stacking

**Marginal and optional — do not force it.** RotoWire's four-year data: the real QB↔top-WR weekly
correlation is ~**+0.31** (not the +0.6 quoted from DFS). A QB+WR stack concentrates roughly
**+1.8 points of weekly ceiling for about −1.6 points of floor.** The ceiling tilts your way, but
it is "measured in a couple of points, not a landslide."

- **Use as a tiebreaker only:** if two QBs are in the same tier and one pairs with a WR you
  already own, take the pairing. **Never reach 1–2 rounds above ADP for a stack in redraft.**
- Best natural stack environments in 2026: Bengals (Burrow + Chase/Higgins), Cowboys (Dak +
  Lamb/Pickens), Bears (C. Williams + Odunze/Burden/Loveland).

## 9. Draft-slot playbooks under 3RR

**Early (slots 1–4)** — e.g. slot 1 picks 1 / 24 / 36. You get an elite anchor but a long wait to
your 2nd and 3rd picks, and **no R2/R3 back-to-back**. Take the best RB (Gibbs/Bijan) or an elite
WR; you'll likely get a WR2 + RB2 in the 21–40 range. Anchor-RB or balanced.
**Do not count on elite RB coming back — it won't.**

**Middle (slots 5–8)** — smoothest cadence, ~10–12 picks between selections. Most flexible;
**let the board come to you (BPA).** A great seat for Modified Zero-RB, or for catching a falling
elite (a tier-1 WR, or Bowers/McBride slipping).

**Late (slots 9–12)** — tight early cluster (slot 12 = 12-13-25; slot 11 = 11-14-26). **Use the
turn to lock two positions at once** — an RB + a WR, two tier-2 WRs, or a WR + Bowers/McBride.
Then you wait ~22 picks, so prioritise high-floor players at the turn and upside later.
LordSkunk's rule: "write your pick numbers through Round 6 and plan around the R2/R3 turn."

## 10. Why third-round reversal matters

Per Sleeper's own support documentation, a standard snake reverses every round; 3RR keeps round
2's direction going for one extra round. **Round 3 runs the same direction as round 2
(back-of-order first), then the board snakes normally from round 4.** There is exactly one flip
and no second reversal. The full pick table is in `draft/PLAYBOOK.md` §3 and
`draft/pick_order.json`.

- In a standard snake, slot 1 gets the 1.01 **and** the 2/3 turn (picks 24-25 back-to-back) — a
  double reward. **3RR removes the second reward:** slot 1 waits until pick 36 for its third pick.
- **The back of round 1 is compensated:** slot 12 gets 12-13 back-to-back then 25 — three of the
  top 25 picks inside a 13-pick window. Slot 11 gets 11-14-26; slot 10 gets 10-15-27.
- Fantasy Index's analysis: 3RR narrows the top-to-bottom spread from ~26 points to ~21. The top
  three slots each lose ~2–3 points of expected value; the bottom slots each gain ~2–3. It
  flattens but does **not** eliminate the early-pick edge — Draft Sharks still rates No. 1 as the
  best half-PPR slot in 2026 on raw player value.
- **Net:** slots 1–2 remain strong (elite anchor), slots 10–12 become genuinely good (tight early
  cluster), and the true "dead" seats are the middle-late — roughly **8–9** — which get neither
  the elite anchor nor the tight turn.
- Context on late-slot viability: ffwrapped's analysis of 580 real 12-team Sleeper leagues found
  picks 7–12 produced **58.1% of 2024 champions**. A single-season sample — directional only, and
  a reminder that the first pick is not automatically the best seat every season.

**The 3RR math is exact and static.** Unlike everything else here, it does not move with news.

## 11. No-K / No-DST consequences

- All 13 picks are QB/RB/WR/TE. **No bench slot is ever spent on a K or DST**, so effectively
  every team runs 6 skill-position bench stashes.
- **Replacement level drops sharply.** With 156 picks all on skill players the waiver pool is
  thinner than in a K/DST league; useful RBs and WRs get rostered ~10–20 picks deeper than their
  K/DST-league ADP would suggest. **Hoard upside.**
- **Only 156 picks get made.** Anything past roughly rank 160 on the board will go undrafted, so
  late-round targets must be genuine upside swings, not depth.
- FLEX is RB/WR/TE-eligible (standard Sleeper), so effective league-wide starting demand is
  ~24 RB + 24 WR + 12 TE + 12 QB + 12 FLEX (the flex splitting mostly RB/WR).

## 13. Known market inefficiencies

**Under-drafted QBs relative to weekly value** — Brock Purdy (drafted around QB18 but with
per-game QB1 upside late in '25), Jaxson Dart, Kyler Murray, Justin Herbert, Trevor Lawrence.
The flat QB curve means all of these are fine to wait for.

**Over-drafted** — mid-tier RBs in the dead zone (R4–7 volume-only backs); Patrick Mahomes (name
value, capped rushing); Tee Higgins at R4 (an injury-prone WR2). Eric Karabell's 2026 "Do Not
Draft" list flags the Giants' young trio as overvalued.

**Under-drafted / value (ADP behind projection)** — Stefon Diggs (WR49 ADP, WR3 role in WAS),
Brian Thomas Jr., David Montgomery, Keenan Allen, Kyle Pitts, Tucker Kraft.

**Structural** — WR is deep and flat in R3–7, so the position is slightly under-drafted in the
middle relative to the RBs going there. RB is scarce and top-heavy, so early RB carries outsized
VORP.

## 15. Staged recommendations

**Stage 0 — before the draft, once the slot is assigned**
1. Write out your exact pick numbers through Round 6 from the table in `PLAYBOOK.md` §3.
2. Slots **1–4**: plan Anchor-RB (Gibbs/Bijan/JT tier) or an elite WR, expecting a long wait.
   Slots **5–8**: plan flexible BPA. Slots **9–12**: plan to double-address at the 2/3 turn.
3. Load the tiered board and mark the cliffs — after the top 2 RB, the top 3 WR, and
   Bowers/McBride.

**Stage 1 — R1–2.** Take the highest-VORP elite (RB or WR). Do not take a QB or a non-elite TE.
Bowers/McBride here at value is defensible but not required.

**Stage 2 — R3–5.** Build the starting core. Grab Bowers/McBride **only** if they fall to
~ADP 40 at your pick; otherwise take WR depth and/or a tier-3/4 RB whose role beats the WR on the
board. Start honouring the dead-zone rule (§6).

**Stage 3 — R6–8.** Fill remaining starters + FLEX. Take your one QB here if a tier-3 value
(Purdy / Lawrence / Dart / Murray / Nix) is present and the skill board is flat. Target the young
TE tier (Loveland / Warren) if you punted TE.

**Stage 4 — R9–13.** Upside only. Contingent handcuffs with standalone value, your own stud's
handcuff, a second QB if punting, and rookie/injury lottery tickets — each with a review date.

**Benchmarks that change the plan**
- Allen slides past ~pick 45 → consider taking him; an elite QB becomes worth it.
- Two of your tier-1/2 RBs are fragile → shift toward Robust-RB, draft handcuffs earlier.
- The WR market goes soft (elites past ADP) → pivot WR-heavy, mine RB value in the dead zone.
- RB tiers 1–2 gone before your R2 → pivot to Zero/Modified-RB.
